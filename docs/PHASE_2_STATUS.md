## Step 2.2 outcome — strict wire contracts and journal epoch

## Step 2.3 outcome — CompanionCrypto primitives and golden vectors

### Implemented scope (new `CompanionCrypto` target, pure primitives)

- **Target:** new `CompanionCrypto` library plus `CompanionCryptoTests` in the root package. It depends only on `CompanionProtocol` and the system CryptoKit/Foundation frameworks — no external dependency, no Keychain, no sockets, platforms unchanged. Package pins are untouched; the ADR's dependency adoption remains Steps 2.4a/2.7.
- **Canonical statement encoding (ADR §11):** every statement leads with the version byte and a length-prefixed ASCII domain separator; fields follow in one fixed order per statement type; integers are fixed-width big-endian; every variable byte/UTF-8 field carries a big-endian `UInt16` byte-length prefix; optionals encode an explicit presence byte. Six distinct versioned contexts exist: pairing transcript, session transcript, rotation statement, the two HKDF frame-key directions, and SAS.
- **Transcripts:** `PairingTranscript` (pairing session, mode, endpoint origin, exact protocol tuple, bootstrap secret, both 256-bit nonces, both 65-byte X9.63 long-term keys, host ID, current TLS SPKI fingerprint) and `SessionTranscript` (session/device IDs, exact tuple, both ephemeral keys and nonces, grant revision, authorized-view epoch, host generation) with validating initializers reusing the Step 2.2 `SecureTransportLimits` bounds; encoding cannot fail after construction.
- **P-256 operations:** strict X9.63 65-byte conversions (wrong length, compressed tag, and off-curve points fail closed); transcript signatures produced and consumed as exactly 64 raw `r||s` bytes — DER or any other length never verifies; ephemeral `P256.KeyAgreement` ECDH against validated peer keys.
- **HKDF-SHA256 key schedule:** input key material is the ECDH shared secret, the salt is the SHA-256 hash of the canonical session transcript, and each direction's info label is the canonical encoding of its domain separator plus the exact negotiated `(major, minor, features)` tuple. Output is two independent 32-byte ChaCha20-Poly1305 directional keys.
- **AEAD frame codec:** 33-byte clear header (version, 4-byte direction prefix, connection ID, big-endian `UInt64` counter, ciphertext length) authenticated in full as additional data; deterministic 96-bit nonce = direction prefix plus big-endian counter; counters start at 0 and receivers accept exactly the next counter. Duplicates, gaps, reflection, unknown direction prefixes, cross-connection frames, header/ciphertext/tag tamper, truncation, trailing bytes, zero-length and oversized payloads, and wrong keys are rejected with closed reason codes; the sealer refuses to seal past `UInt64.max - 1` and both codec halves fail closed permanently after any violation — counters never wrap.
- **SPKI fingerprints:** SHA-256 over the P-256 SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER of a strictly validated key (clean reimplementation; no spike code imported).
- **SAS derivation:** HKDF-SHA256 with its own versioned canonical label over the pairing-transcript hash; nine output bytes of which exactly the first 66 bits are consumed as six big-endian 11-bit indices — each uniform over `0..<2048` with no modulo bias — rendered as canonical zero-padded four-digit decimal groups (for example `1810-0125-1393-2037-1338-1258`).
- **Rotation statements:** canonical encoding with strict decoding (wrong version, wrong domain, wrong field lengths, truncation, and trailing bytes fail closed) and pure fail-closed verification in fixed order: host signature over the canonical bytes, current-SPKI pin binding, strict generation monotonicity above the caller's last accepted generation, then the inclusive validity interval at an injected epoch-seconds time. Persistence of pins and generations stays with later steps.

### Recorded decision — SAS word-list rendering deferred to Step 2.13

The plan fixes the SAS as six words from a fixed versioned 2,048-word list. Step 2.3 fixes everything security-relevant about it — the 66-bit uniform derivation, the versioned `codex-micro/sas/v1` context, and the six final 11-bit indices — and exposes the canonical decimal-group rendering. Binding the indices to the fixed versioned word list is recorded as a Step 2.13 UI concern (shared with the Step 2.14 acceptance host) so that a complete, reviewed word list ships once rather than a hand-assembled one. Adding the word list later changes display only; the derived indices and their golden vectors are already final.

### Step 2.3 verification

- 93 new deterministic tests in `CompanionCryptoTests` (root suite total 238): golden vectors are byte-exact hex fixtures for the pairing/session/rotation canonical encodings and hashes, HKDF directional keys, sealed frames for counters 0 and 1, SAS indices/display, the ECDH shared secret, and the SPKI fingerprint; recorded host signatures verify against the fixtures.
- Client and server roles are exercised independently throughout: transcripts built on each side are byte-identical, one role signs while the other verifies, ECDH and the key schedule agree across roles, and one role seals while the other opens.
- Every pairing-transcript, session-transcript, and rotation-statement field mutated independently fails signature verification (and every pairing mutation changes the SAS); `mode` is the one unmutatable field because its Phase 2 vocabulary has a single case.
- Adversarial frame coverage: every one of the 33 header bytes tampered individually plus per-region closed-reason checks, ciphertext and tag tamper, reflection, wrong-direction key, grafted header (AAD binding), duplicate, gap, cross-connection replay, sender and receiver counter exhaustion before wrap, overflow-sentinel counter, truncation, trailing bytes, oversized declared length, zero-length payload, wrong key, wrong key length, and permanent fail-closed latching.

- The SPKI golden fingerprint was cross-checked outside the codebase: the identical SubjectPublicKeyInfo DER parses as a valid EC key under OpenSSL and its independent SHA-256 digest matches the fixture.

### Honest limitations

- **Pure primitives only.** No Keychain, Secure Enclave, storage, socket, listener, Bonjour, pairing endpoint, or command path was added; nothing in this step transports real traffic or holds authority. Integration arrives with Steps 2.4a–2.7.
- **No choreography.** Which endpoint signs which transcript-bound statement at which protocol step, claim/consume ordering, and confirmation flows are Step 2.5/2.6 scope; those steps may add further domain-separated statement types under this contract without changing the existing layouts.

## Step 2.4b outcome — Mac-authoritative device-grant authority

### Implemented scope (production `MacBridgeCore`)

- **Authoritative records:** `AuthoritativeDeviceGrant` stores the opaque UUID device ID, strictly validated 65-byte uncompressed/on-curve P-256 X9.63 public key, display-neutral `UInt64` created/last-seen epoch seconds, the existing `DeviceCapability` set, bounded opaque project allowlist in canonical NFC bytes (empty by default), existing `MobileActionProfile` ceiling, per-device grant revision and authorized-view epoch (both starting at 1), optional epoch-seconds expiry, and a permanent typed revocation/expiry tombstone. Tombstoned IDs can never be granted again; a new pairing must create a new device ID. Host generation and the anti-rollback authority write sequence are `UInt64` and never wrap.
- **Canonical bounded blob:** `GrantAuthorityBlobCodec` encodes one version-1, domain-separated `codex-micro/device-grant-authority/v1` blob with fixed-width big-endian counters, `UInt16` length prefixes, explicit optional-presence bytes, byte-canonical ordering of device records/capabilities/projects, and strict rejection of trailing bytes, duplicate/non-canonical device or set entries, unknown closed-vocabulary values, malformed keys/timestamps/counters, truncation, foreign version/domain, and blobs over 64 KiB. Encode validates every count/variable length before conversion and enforces the 64 KiB ceiling incrementally, so no narrowing conversion can trap or truncate.
- **Synchronous storage seam:** `GrantAuthorityStorage` is explicitly synchronous, `Sendable`, and thread-safe because `DeviceGrantAuthority` loads during actor initialization. It distinguishes one explicit zero-byte fresh-install marker from a missing item; missing, duplicate, corrupt, oversized, rollback, or store failure is a LAN-disable condition. `DataProtectionKeychainGrantStore` uses one fixed generic-password service/account, `kSecUseDataProtectionKeychain`, `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly`, non-synchronizable ThisDeviceOnly semantics, and whole-item `SecItemAdd`/`SecItemUpdate`. The duplicate path verifies exact cardinality *and* protection attributes before update and exact persisted bytes afterward, and the update predicate retains those attributes so a replacement race cannot redirect the write to a weaker item; a nonempty write may never recreate a deleted item, which surfaces authority loss instead of silently restoring continuity. No file store or separate encryption key exists.
- **Single authority actor:** `DeviceGrantAuthority` is the sole API for session authentication and command authorization. It loads at initialization, exposes a closed availability state, accepts pairing output through `addGrant`, and owns revoke, explicit expiry, strict project-scope reduction, capability/profile amendment, last-seen touch, host-generation advance, reload, and current-grant lookup. Every mutation increments the authority sequence and persists the complete canonical blob before publishing new in-memory state. A persistence failure leaves the prior state unpublished, latches the authority unavailable, and denies every later call until successful reload; sequence rollback or same-sequence state substitution is rejected against the in-memory high-water mark.
- **Version rules:** every grant mutation advances the device revision; project-scope reduction and revocation/expiry also advance the authorized-view epoch. Capability/profile amendment advances revision and advances view epoch exactly when `.view` membership changes. Last-seen changes advance only the authority sequence. Host generation changes only through explicit global invalidation. Overflow of revision, view epoch, host generation, or authority sequence latches the authority closed instead of wrapping.
- **Expiry contract:** clock-checked lookups deny at and after `expiresAt` immediately. `expire(deviceID:)` is the only persisted expiry-tombstone transition and has the same revision/view-epoch semantics as revocation while preserving a distinct closed reason. The active-session expiry scheduler added with session/observe integration must call this API before closing affected sessions; this step intentionally creates no wall-clock sleeping task and makes no active-session claim.
- **Phase 1 policy bridge and isolation:** `effectivePolicyGrant` derives the existing `DeviceGrant` so all command decisions continue through `CapabilityPolicy.authorize`; no policy logic is duplicated. Authenticated session code receives a `DeviceGrantHandle` bound to one device ID and exposing no device-ID parameter, so that handle cannot read or touch another device. The only bulk snapshot is explicitly named/documented for Mac administration and includes tombstones.
- **No new target, dependency, package, socket, listener, Bonjour, pairing endpoint, command path, or free-form logging** was added. `MacBridgeCore` remains the sole owner of device-grant authority.

## Step 2.5 outcome — transport-independent pairing state machine

### Implemented scope

- **One new wire message (`CompanionProtocol`):** `SecurePairingConfirmation` (device → host) carries the pairing session ID, the device-asserted device ID, and the device's 64-byte raw `r||s` transcript signature. It completes the mutual signing Step 2.2 deliberately deferred: the device signs exactly the canonical pairing transcript the host signed in `SecurePairingResponse`. Decoding is strict (unknown fields, wrong types, malformed UUIDs, and any non-64-byte signature fail closed) and the validating initializer keeps out-of-bounds values unrepresentable. No existing message changed.
- **QR payload (`PairingQRPayload`):** exactly the ADR §12 allowlist — canonical version, exact protocol/feature selection, opaque host ID, normalized endpoint origin, host-identity SPKI fingerprint, current TLS SPKI fingerprint, pairing session ID, 256-bit one-time secret, and expiry — encoded under its own canonical domain `codex-micro/pairing-qr/v1`. There is **no `hostName`** or any display, device, user, or project field. Strict decoding rejects a wrong version, a foreign domain, wrong field lengths, unknown/duplicate/misordered/empty feature sets, truncation, trailing bytes, and any origin that is not already normalized.
- **Endpoint-origin normalization (`PairingEndpointOrigin`):** total, deterministic normalization — lowercased scheme (`wss` only) and host, strict dotted-quad IPv4, bracketed IPv6 rendered in RFC 5952 canonical form (lowercase, no leading zeros, longest zero run compressed, leftmost on a tie), default port 443 elided. Hostnames, wildcard addresses, IPv4-mapped IPv6, embedded-IPv4 and zone-ID IPv6 spellings, bracket/family mismatches, userinfo, paths, queries, fragments, and out-of-range or leading-zero ports fail closed with a closed error vocabulary. Pairing compares endpoints by normalized equality only.
- **Host coordinator (`PairingCoordinator`, actor):** injected wall clock, monotonic clock, CSPRNG, ephemeral session store, and long-term signer seams. `createSession` draws all three session values from the CSPRNG — a 128-bit pairing session ID plus the 256-bit bootstrap secret and 256-bit host nonce — records `available`, and sets expiry to exactly created + 300 s. `claim(request:)` runs the whole decision — lifecycle check, expiry check, constant-time secret comparison, and the `available → claimed` commit — inside **one store-level critical section**, before any transcript or signature processing. Mode, endpoint origin, selection, and device public key are validated only afterwards, so a completed claim spends the single-use secret regardless of the eventual outcome. Losing concurrent claimants (`sessionAlreadyClaimed`), reuse after success or failure (`secretAlreadyConsumed`), expiry, cancellation, and the bounded per-session failed-claim budget (`attemptLimitExceeded`, 5 attempts) are each rejected with a distinct closed reason.
- **Atomicity lives in the store, not the actor.** `PairingSessionStore` exposes a compare-and-swap primitive (`withRecord(_:_:)`) whose body runs under the store's own mutual exclusion, and every state decision — the claim, both confirmations, cancellation, and each terminal transition — happens inside it. Actor isolation alone would only serialize one coordinator: Step 2.7 is expected to create one coordinator per connection over a shared store, and that configuration must still yield exactly one winner per QR secret.
- **Monotonic expiry.** The five-minute lifetime is enforced against an injected monotonic source (default: `ContinuousClock`, which never moves backwards and also advances while the machine sleeps). The wall clock is used only for the `expiresAt` the QR displays and the pairing instant in the proposal, so an NTP step, a manual clock change, or a VM snapshot restore can neither extend nor shorten a session's usable life.
- **Expiry sweep.** `sweepExpired()` clears the bootstrap secret, host nonce, and transcript from every record that reached its monotonic expiry, and removes records that also passed `expiredRecordRetentionSeconds` (300 s) so the exact closed reason survives for a window before the record disappears. Every public coordinator operation sweeps first, which bounds accumulation under ordinary traffic; the Step 2.13 assembly must additionally call it on a timer so an abandoned session that nothing else touches cannot hold material indefinitely.
- **Redacted secret-bearing types.** `PairingTranscript`, `PairingQRPayload`, `PairingSessionRecord`, `PairingClaimAcceptance`, and `PairingDeviceVerification` render as `…(redacted)` and expose no secret through reflection: four use an empty mirror, and the QR payload's mirror lists its allowlisted field names with a placeholder in place of the secret, so `print`, string interpolation, `dump`, and crash-path reflection cannot surface the bootstrap secret. The confirmation methods are **not** `@discardableResult`: a completed result carries the only `PairedDeviceProposal`, and the transcript is cleared immediately afterwards.
- **Mutual transcript signatures:** the host builds the Step 2.3 `PairingTranscript` from the request plus its own contributions, signs it through the injected `PairingTranscriptSigner` seam (the real Secure Enclave signer stays in `MacBridgeServer` and is never imported), and returns `SecurePairingResponse`. The device-side reference implementation pins the host identity fingerprint from the QR, verifies the host signature over an independently reconstructed transcript, derives the verification phrase, and — only after its own user confirms — signs the same transcript into `SecurePairingConfirmation`, which the host verifies against the device key bound in the transcript.
- **SAS and dual confirmation:** both endpoints derive the phrase with the existing `SecureShortAuthenticationString` (66 uniformly derived bits). Pairing completes only after **both** local confirmations — the Mac user's phrase confirmation and the device's confirmation message — in either order. One-sided confirmation returns `awaitingConfirmation` and completes nothing; a phrase mismatch, an invalid device signature, cancellation, or expiry completes nothing and spends the attempt.
- **Outcome and initial authorization:** success returns exactly one `PairedDeviceProposal` (pairing session, device ID, validated device public key, initial grant, pairing instant). The initial grant is the only constructible `PairedDeviceGrantIntent` — `observe` capability with an empty project allowlist — expressed in crypto-local enums, since `CompanionCrypto` must not depend on `MacBridgeCore`; the Mac assembly maps `observe` onto `DeviceCapability.view` plus the `observe` mobile action profile when it calls `DeviceGrantAuthority.addGrant`. Any failure returns a closed reason and **no proposal**; this step stores nothing.
- **Closed vocabulary and content-free errors:** every rejection is a case of `PairingClosedReason` or `PairingEndpointOriginError`. No free-form string, wire content, endpoint text, key material, or secret-derived value appears in any error. Terminal transitions clear the bootstrap secret, host nonce, and transcript from the ephemeral record.
- **No new target, dependency, package, socket, listener, Bonjour advertisement, pairing endpoint, Keychain access, grant store, or Codex path** was added. `CompanionCrypto` still imports only Foundation, CryptoKit, and `CompanionProtocol`.

- 80 new deterministic tests (root suite total 437): 76 in `CompanionCryptoTests` (14 endpoint-origin, 12 QR payload/redaction, 7 entropy/constant-time/policy, 25 coordinator lifecycle, 18 end-to-end choreography) and 4 in `MacBridgeCoreTests` (1 golden JSON fixture plus 3 adversarial decoding tests for `SecurePairingConfirmation`).

### Honest limitations

- **No transport.** Nothing in this step opens a socket, listener, WebSocket, or Bonjour advertisement, and no pairing endpoint is reachable. Delivering the three pairing messages, the ADR §9 per-source pairing-attempt rate limits, and handshake deadlines are Step 2.7 scope; the per-session failed-claim budget implemented here is separate from those transport limits.
- **Seams, not integrations.** The host signer is a protocol: the Secure Enclave `.host` identity in `MacBridgeServer` is wired to it by later assembly, and no test in this environment produced a Secure Enclave signature. Grant persistence is likewise deferred — pairing returns a proposal and `DeviceGrantAuthority.addGrant` is called by the assembly, so **no grant is stored by this step, on success or failure**.
- **Ephemeral, memory-only sessions.** The production store is in-memory by design; a bridge restart cancels every pending pairing and no bootstrap secret ever reaches disk. There is no cross-process pairing state, and a custom store implementation must honour the documented compare-and-swap contract for the claim to stay atomic.
- **Sweeping is opportunistic, not scheduled.** This step adds no timer or background task; `sweepExpired()` runs only when a public coordinator operation is called or a caller invokes it. Wiring the periodic call is a Step 2.13 assembly obligation.
- **Zeroing is best-effort at the language level.** Terminal transitions and the sweep replace `Data` values, which removes the reference the process holds; Swift makes no guarantee that the freed heap bytes are wiped or never paged. This is the same property the rest of the codebase relies on and is not a claim of guaranteed memory scrubbing.
- **Device-side expiry is advisory.** The device checks the QR's `expiresAt` against its own wall clock because no monotonic reference is shared across endpoints. The host's monotonic enforcement is the authority; a device with a skewed clock can start an attempt the host then rejects.
- **The device side is a reference implementation** for deterministic tests and the Step 2.14 acceptance host, not the iOS product client. It models the device's local user confirmation as the act of producing the confirmation message.
- **Device ID is device-asserted.** The confirmation carries the device's non-secret identifier, which the transcript layout fixed in Step 2.3 does not cover. It is never treated as proof of identity (threat model §3.3); uniqueness, tombstone permanence, and duplicate rejection remain the Mac grant authority's decision at `addGrant`.
- The verification phrase is still rendered as decimal groups; the fixed versioned word list remains the recorded Step 2.13 UI concern, and the derived indices are unchanged.
- Source-only evidence: no listener, loopback, simulator, generic-device, or physical-device claim is made by Step 2.5. The generic iOS device build of `CompanionProtocol`/`CompanionCrypto` was not run in this environment and remains required merge evidence.

## Step 2.6 outcome — transport-independent authenticated session handshake

### Implemented scope (production `CompanionCrypto`, no new target or dependency)

- **Mac-authority seam (`SessionAuthoritySnapshot`, `SessionAuthorityProviding`).** One read returns the device's stored long-term public key, grant revision, authorized-view epoch, host-wide generation, closed liveness (`live`/`revoked`/`expired`), and remaining grant lifetime. The coordinator copies the three counters into the session transcript and the signed response **from this snapshot alone** (plan §2 invariant 1). The wire request deliberately carries no counter field, so there is no value for a phone to present; a device that insists on its remembered counters simply fails to verify the host's signature. A throwing authority denies authentication outright (`authorityUnavailable`, invariant 15) — there is no cached or memory-only fallback — and a snapshot answering for a different device is rejected (`authorityInconsistent`). `InMemorySessionAuthority` is the deterministic implementation for tests and the Step 2.14 acceptance host, with explicit mutators for revocation, expiry, revision/view/host-generation advance, remaining lifetime, and authority loss; its counter mutators fail the authority closed at `UInt64.max` instead of wrapping.
- **New canonical statement (`SessionAuthenticationStatement`, domain `codex-micro/session-auth-statement/v1`).** The device cannot sign the full `SessionTranscript` first, because the transcript binds the Mac's grant revision, authorized-view epoch, and host generation, and disclosing those to an unauthenticated client would leak grant metadata (plan §7 gate 2). The device therefore signs an eighth canonical statement binding the host ID, its asserted device ID, the exact selection, and its two fresh contributions; the host verifies it against the authority's stored device key and only then signs the complete transcript back. Both endpoints still derive frame keys from the full transcript hash, so any disagreement about the transcript produces unusable keys. The statement's SHA-256 is recorded with the session, so a byte-identical replay cannot silently supersede the live session it was captured from (`authenticationReplayed`).
- **Host coordinator (`SessionCoordinator`, actor).** Injected authority, long-term signer, session registry, CSPRNG, and monotonic clock. `authenticate(request:connectionID:)` reads the authority, requires the selection to equal the one supported tuple, verifies the device statement signature, draws a fresh 128-bit session ID, a fresh 256-bit host nonce, and a fresh ephemeral ECDH key from the CSPRNG seam, builds the transcript from the request plus its own contributions plus the authority snapshot, signs it through the seam, derives directional frame keys with the existing Step 2.3 schedule, and registers the session last. Every failure — unknown, revoked, expired, unavailable or inconsistent authority, rejected selection, invalid device key or signature, unavailable host signature, entropy failure, unrepresentable grant lifetime, replayed statement — returns a distinct closed reason and leaves **no session and no keys**.
- **Exact negotiation.** The accepted selection is `SessionPolicy.requiredSelection()`: the exact supported major, the exact supported minor, and the complete supported feature set. A downgrade (absent feature), a future minor, a foreign major, and an unknown feature are each rejected, and Phase 2 defines no approval feature to negotiate.
- **One active session per device (invariant 18).** `AuthenticatedSessionStore` exposes a mutate-under-lock compare-and-swap primitive over the whole registry state; the replacement, the generation advance, and every invalidation happen inside it, so atomicity lives at the store rather than in actor-instance isolation. Successful reauthentication atomically replaces the device's session and returns the superseded identity for the caller to close.
- **Authentication generation productionized (ADR §16 row 2.6).** The spike's model-only lifecycle generation is now the value every session records. `advanceAuthenticationGeneration()` — listener restart, global invalidation, host-generation change — returns every invalidated identity and empties the registry, and a record that predates the current generation is denied (`generationStale`) and swept. At the fixed width it fails closed rather than wrapping: the registry is emptied, the coordinator latches, and every later authentication and validation is denied (`generationExhausted`).
- **Continued-use checks.** `validate(_:)` requires the session to be the device's current one, in the current generation, inside its monotonic expiry deadline, and matching the device's *current* authority counters; a newer grant revision, authorized-view epoch, or host generation denies continued use (`authorizationChanged`, invariant 11). It re-checks the registry after the authority read so a concurrent reauthentication cannot leave a validation outliving its session.
- **Authorization-change, expiry, and sweep primitives.** `invalidateSessions(forDevice:)` (revocation/reduction/expiry), `close(_:)` (exact identity only), and `sweepInvalidSessions()` each return the affected identities — the sweep pairs each with the closed reason the transport must close it with — so Step 2.7/2.8 can close exactly the right connections. Active-session expiry is derived once, at authentication, from the grant's *remaining lifetime* onto the injected monotonic seam, so no wall-clock movement in either direction can extend or shorten a live session.
- **Reconnect isolation.** Every authentication mints a fresh session ID, fresh ephemeral keys, a fresh nonce, fresh directional keys, and per-direction counters starting at 0. The frame header's connection binding is the session ID — the one identifier both endpoints know — so a frame from a previous session cannot open on a new one in either direction.
- **Device-side reference flow (`SessionDeviceEndpoint`, `SessionDeviceAttempt`).** Mirrors Step 2.5's pairing flow: it draws its own nonce and ephemeral key, signs the statement, and then verifies the reply against the host identity pinned at pairing over an independently reconstructed transcript before deriving the identical directional keys. Because the reconstruction binds the reply's counters, a host — or anything on the path — that echoes counters other than the ones it signed fails closed.
- **Closed vocabulary and redaction.** Every rejection is a `SessionClosedReason` case, each mapped to a closed `SecureCloseReason` wire code with pre-authentication failures collapsed onto `authenticationFailed`. No error carries wire content, key material, counters, or free-form text. `EstablishedHostSession`, `EstablishedDeviceSession`, `HostSessionAuthentication`, and `SessionDeviceAttempt` render as `…(redacted)` with empty mirrors, so `print`, interpolation, `dump`, and crash-path reflection cannot surface frame keys or the attempt-local ephemeral key.
- **No new target, dependency, package, socket, listener, Bonjour advertisement, endpoint, Keychain access, grant store, or Codex path** was added. `CompanionCrypto` still imports only Foundation, CryptoKit, and `CompanionProtocol`.

### Step 2.6 verification

- 45 new deterministic tests (root suite total 482), all in `CompanionCryptoTests`: 31 host-coordinator tests and 14 end-to-end choreography/frame tests. Clocks, monotonic clocks, CSPRNG, registry store, authority, and both signers are injected; no test sleeps.

- **No transport or listener.** Nothing here opens a socket, WebSocket, or listener; the ADR §9 handshake deadlines, connection caps, and rate limits, and the actual delivery of the two authentication messages, are Step 2.7. - **One-shot lifecycle and registry-based teardown (ADR §14).** `idle → starting → running → stopping → terminated`, with no restart after stop or partial startup failure. Because NIOTS does not cascade a listener close to its children, teardown is registry-driven: it stops accepting, closes every tracked child, closes the server channel, and shuts the event-loop group down, attempting every step even when an earlier one fails, and reporting `cleanupFailed` if anything is left behind. `publishing` exists in the phase vocabulary because ADR §14 fixes the shape of the machine; **nothing in this step ever enters it**.
- **Exact upgrade policy (`ListenerUpgradePolicy`, ADR §9).** HTTP/1.1 `GET` only, one fixed path with any query rejected, exact `Origin` and subprotocol, WebSocket 13 with a 16-byte decoded key, a lowercased header-name allowlist with single occurrence for every required header, and 16 fields / 8 KiB total / 4 KiB per field enforced both by the NIO decoder limits and independently by the policy. Compression is never negotiated: exactly the `URLSessionWebSocketTask` `permessage-deflate` offer is tolerated, omitted from the response, and RSV bits are rejected at the frame layer. The success result is exactly one response header.
- **Frame policy (`ListenerFragmentAggregator`, `ListenerBinaryFramePolicy`).** Binary application frames only; 16 KiB decoder frame cap, 64 KiB message cap, 8 fragments. Text frames, reserved bits, unexpected continuations, a new message during fragmentation, empty non-final fragments, fragment-count and aggregate-size overflow, oversized control frames, and unknown opcodes each close the connection with a closed violation code. Control frames interleave with fragmentation as RFC 6455 requires.
- **Every ADR §9 Step 2.7 ceiling, which the spike implemented none of.** `ListenerCeilings` carries the values and `validated()` refuses any configuration that **exceeds** the ADR (tightening is allowed) *or* is mutually inconsistent. `ListenerAdmissionController` enforces 16 global, 4 concurrent unauthenticated, 6 per-source connections/minute, and the two per-source handshake windows on an injected monotonic clock; `ListenerInboundRateMeter` enforces 32 messages/s and 1 MiB/s per connection; `ListenerOutboundBoundHandler` enforces 64 frames / 256 KiB with a 10 s non-writable close; `ListenerKeepAliveHandler` enforces the 30 s ping cadence, 10 s matching-pong deadline, and 120 s post-upgrade application idle expiry; `ListenerUpgradeDeadlineHandler` and `ListenerHandshakeGateHandler` enforce the 10 s TLS+upgrade and 20 s authentication deadlines. All timing runs on injected clock/timer seams, so every ceiling test is deterministic.
- **Pipeline order is part of the contract.** Head to tail the post-upgrade chain is outbound bound → fragment aggregator → inbound rate meter → keep-alive → binary frame policy → pre-authentication gate. That order is what makes the ceilings real rather than nominal: the outbound bound is head-most so **every** outbound frame is accounted for — application payloads, the pongs the frame policy emits in reply to peer pings, and the server's own keep-alive pings — and the rate meter sits ahead of the handler that consumes control frames so peer-driven pings and pongs consume the inbound message and byte ceilings. The keep-alive handler writes its pings from its own position toward the head; writing through the channel would start at the tail and hand a `WebSocketFrame` to a handler whose outbound type is `ByteBuffer`, which traps the process.
- **Server keep-alive starts at authentication.** An unauthenticated connection is bounded by the much stricter 20 s authentication deadline, so no server-originated traffic is emitted before it authenticates, and `validated()` separately refuses a cadence tightened to inside that deadline. Every ping carries a fresh 8-byte counter payload and only a pong echoing exactly those bytes clears the deadline, so an unsolicited or stale pong cannot keep a dead peer alive. Only inbound **binary** frames reset the application idle timer — control traffic and text frames do not.
- **Pre-upgrade traffic is bounded (a tightening beyond the ADR).** NIO consults the upgrade policy only when an `Upgrade` header is present, so a plain `GET`/`POST` never reaches `ListenerUpgradePolicy` and the post-upgrade meters never see it. `ListenerPreUpgradeByteLimitHandler` sits ahead of the HTTP decoder and bounds raw bytes per connection before the upgrade, then removes itself. Disclosure was zero either way; this closes a bandwidth and CPU channel.
- **The per-source limiter map is bounded.** Admission runs at accept, before TLS, so an attacker cycling IPv6 privacy addresses would otherwise add a permanent entry per connection while the per-source ceiling never engaged. The map is pruned on the production admission paths themselves once it passes a soft threshold and is hard capped, evicting least-recently-active entries first.
- **Pre-authentication allowlist (`ListenerPreAuthAllowlist`, plan §7 gate 2).** Before authentication a connection may carry only the four device-originated handshake kinds, one dispatch at a time. The transport-owned envelope decodes strictly (unknown fields, unknown kinds, host-only kinds inbound, empty and oversized bodies all fail). **Exactly one closed reason is observable before authentication**: the gate collapses its own refusals *and* whatever reason the handshake seam returns, so a malformed body, an application message, an exhausted per-source ceiling, a failed pairing, and a failed authentication are one outcome. The per-source ceiling is the reason this matters — it is evaluated before any coordinator is consulted and its counter is shared by every peer behind one source address, so a distinct rate-limited reason would let one device learn that another behind the same address recently attempted pairing. Encoding uses sorted keys, so those refusals are byte-identical on the wire. Specific reasons resume only after authentication.
- **Both messages of both handshake flows are charged.** Charging only the first message left the rest free, so a source could spend a whole authentication deadline on signature verifications across every unauthenticated slot. Pairing messages are charged against the ADR §9 per-source pairing ceiling; authentication messages are charged against the per-source connection ceiling, which introduces no number the ADR does not already fix.
- **Handshake seam (`ListenerHandshakeHandling`).** The whole surface between the transport and the Step 2.5/2.6 state machines. `DenyingListenerHandshakeHandler` is the fail-closed default — an unconfigured listener authenticates nobody — and `CoordinatorListenerHandshakeHandler` wires the real `PairingCoordinator` and `SessionCoordinator`. `MacBridgeServer` still does **not** import `MacBridgeCore`, so the executor-bypass seam is intact.
- **Default-off and fail-closed startup (invariants 2/5, gate 1).** `ListenerConfiguration` has a private initializer and exactly two factories: `disabled(binding:)` and `testOnlyEnabled(...)`. There is no user-facing enable control, no production Bonjour in any form, and no Codex command path. Startup runs independent injected prerequisite probes in a fixed order — enablement, Codex support, authorization policy, grant authority, TLS identity — then bind revalidation and live-interface pinning, and any one failure terminates the listener with its own closed `ListenerStartupFailure`.
- **Deterministic pinned client (`PinnedProbeWebSocketClient`).** SPKI-pinned `URLSessionWebSocketTask` with no CA or system trust evaluation, surfacing an exact `.pinMismatch` rather than a generic URLSession error, plus a `withConnection` API so a multi-message handshake stays on the single transport connection the host binds handshake state to.
- **Closed-code logging (`ListenerLogCode`, 24 cases).** The listener's only logging API accepts a closed code and a nonnegative integer count; no free-form string, endpoint, address, header, certificate, fingerprint, frame byte, device ID, or payload can reach a sink.

### Honest limitations

- **Loopback and Mac-only evidence. Never physical-device proof.** Every integration result was produced against `127.0.0.1` on one development Mac. No iPhone, no real Wi-Fi, no LAN, no IP change, and no multi-network behaviour is proven here; that is Step 2.14 and remains gated.
- **TLS version, cipher, and ticket behaviour on the wire is not observed by this step's tests.** The 1.3-only, single-suite, no-tickets, no-resumption, no-False-Start, no-fallback configuration is set through public Network.framework API, and this step's tests assert only that the options build and that a pinned handshake completes. **Nothing here captures or parses a ClientHello/ServerHello or attempts a resumption.** The evidence that this configuration produces those properties on the wire is the merged transport spike (`Spikes/Phase2Transport`, PRs #8/#9/#10, recorded in ADR §5), which is signed-Mac loopback and one-LAN observation — not stability, multi-network, or physical-device proof. If any of those Network.framework behaviours regress, this step's suite would not detect it.
- **The served TLS key is not a Secure Enclave key in tests.** `swift test` has no entitled signed host, so the loopback tests serve a process-local non-persistent `SecKey`. The production path (`ListenerServingIdentity.init(certificate:identity:)`) goes through `BridgeTLSIdentityAssembly`, which refuses anything that is not Secure Enclave-backed, but that production path was **not** exercised in this environment.
- **Availability: 4 unauthenticated peers can deny the whole LAN feature.** Every connection is admitted unauthenticated, so the ADR §9 ceiling of 4 concurrent unauthenticated connections is also the total number of *simultaneous* peers that can be mid-handshake. Four cheap connections that simply hold their slots for the 20 s authentication deadline block **every** new connection, including legitimate pairing, and repeating that costs an attacker nothing beyond the 6-per-source-per-minute connection ceiling — which a handful of source addresses defeats. This is a faithful implementation of the ADR's own 16/4 numbers, not a deviation from them, and disclosure stays zero throughout; but it is a complete unauthenticated denial of the feature. Mitigations exist (reserving unauthenticated slots per source, admitting only one in-flight handshake per source, shortening the deadline once bytes stop arriving, or preferring sources with an existing grant) and all of them change the ADR's numbers or add new ones. **This must be revisited before Step 2.13 makes the listener user-reachable.**
- **No Bonjour.** Step 2.7 adds no advertisement in any form. The `publishing` phase is unreachable and `bonjourPublished` is always `false`. Step 2.13 owns advertisement, the `NWListener.service` caveat re-verification, and the user-facing enable control.
- **No Codex command path and no observe/replay.** The Codex prerequisite is a yes/no probe. Authenticated connections are admitted, counted, and kept alive, but nothing is delivered to them: sealed post-authentication traffic, snapshots, replay, and acknowledgements are Step 2.8, and the command gateway is Step 2.9. `MacBridgeServer` still does not import `MacBridgeCore`.
- **The listener is off by default and enabled only by internal test configuration.** `ListenerConfiguration.testOnlyEnabled` is the sole enabled factory and has no user-reachable caller. Nothing in `CodexMicroBridge` can start a listener.
- **Pairing is wired but not completed at the transport edge.** `CoordinatorListenerHandshakeHandler` forwards pairing messages to `PairingCoordinator` and enforces the per-source pairing ceiling, but pairing still completes only with the Mac user's local phrase confirmation, which is Step 2.13 UI. Pairing never authenticates a connection.
- **Live-interface pinning is proven fail-closed, not proven positive.** The negative path (a production binding with no resolvable live object refuses to start) is tested. A positive `NWParameters.requiredInterface` bind on a real Wi-Fi/Ethernet interface was not exercised, because the deterministic suite binds loopback only.
- **The slow-consumer close is transport-level.** Step 2.8 may substitute a filtered-snapshot fallback above it, as ADR §9 allows; this step only closes.
- **Per-connection rate windows are in-memory and per-process.** A bridge restart resets every window and every admission count, and the per-source key is the peer's numeric address, so sources behind one NAT share a window — which is exactly why every pre-authentication refusal is collapsed onto one reason.
- **Timing side channels are not addressed.** Pre-authentication *reasons* are indistinguishable, but a refusal that never reaches a coordinator returns faster than one that verifies a signature. Constant-time refusal is not attempted at the transport edge.
- **Generic iOS device build not run.** `CompanionProtocol`/`CompanionCrypto` remain iOS-buildable in principle; that build was not run in this environment and stays required merge evidence. `MacBridgeServer` is Mac-only by design.

## Step 2.8 outcome — scoped observe/replay and linearizable revocation

### Implemented scope

Merged as five stacked PRs (#29–#33) in dependency order; no new dependency, target, package, Bonjour advertisement, network mutation, or Codex request was added, and `MacBridgeServer` still does not import `MacBridgeCore`.

- **Observation payload schemas (`CompanionProtocol`, #29).** `ObservedThreadState`, `SecureObservationSnapshot`, `SecureObservationEvent`, and `SecureObservationEventBatch` bind the opaque bytes `SecureObservationDelivery` carries. They are host-originated only but decode as strictly as every other secure message, bound every opaque identifier, and enforce sorted/deduplicated threads and a strictly increasing event run. Two deliberate exclusions: **no approval content** — Phase 2 negotiates no approval feature and its gateway rejects `resolveApproval`, so a device that cannot act on an approval is told nothing about one, and request IDs, digests, item/turn identifiers, and available decisions never reach the observation surface — and **no host journal sequence**, because its movement would reveal activity in projects the device may not see. Every instant is display-neutral `UInt64` epoch seconds, so the wire never depends on a `Date`-encoding strategy chosen at a call site.
- **Fail-closed project attribution (#29).** `ThreadProjectAttributing` resolves an opaque thread to the opaque project authorization is decided against. Returning `nil` means the thread is visible to **no** device — not to one with an empty allowlist, and not to one allowed every project it knows about. The Phase 1 app-server surface this bridge consumes carries no project or workspace attribution, so `DeniedThreadProjectAttribution` is the default and the observation surface discloses nothing until the Mac assembly supplies a resolver (Step 2.13).
- **Per-device authorized view (#29).** `AuthorizedViewScope` derives disclosure scope from the Mac-authoritative grant alone and additionally requires `.view`, so pairing's default grant discloses no project. `DeviceAuthorizedView` owns the device's **private sequence namespace**: a change the device may not see is not merely filtered out of delivery — it never receives a sequence value at all, so the numbers the device observes stay contiguous and reveal nothing about how much unauthorized activity occurred (plan §2 invariant 5). This is why the projection cannot be a filter over the shared journal's sequence values. Retention is a bounded ring; a cursor older than what remains is retention-stale and receives a snapshot rather than a partial history. Adopting a scope whose authorized-view epoch moved purges the retained history and restarts the namespace.
- **Filtered snapshots (#29).** `AuthorizedSnapshotProjection` drops out-of-scope and unattributed threads entirely — not redacted, not counted, not represented — and fails closed rather than truncating when the wire's thread bound is exceeded, because a silently short snapshot is indistinguishable from a complete one. `SystemJournalEpochMint` draws the fresh 128-bit per-process journal epoch.
- **Subscription broker (`MacBridgeCore`, #30).** `DeviceObservationBroker` holds one view and one subscription per device and hands the transport nothing but already-filtered `AuthorizedObservationBatch` values. **Scope is re-read on every operation** — `synchronizedView` is the single point where an authorization change takes effect and it runs before every disclosure decision, so no cached authorization answer sits between a commit and a disclosure. **Back-pressure, not gaps:** a subscription stops producing event batches once 128 events are unacknowledged, and a device that falls past the retention ring is resynchronized by a fresh filtered snapshot — the slow-consumer fallback ADR §9 allows above the transport's own close policy. Acknowledgements are evaluated through `ReplayCursorEnvelope.evaluate`: a resynchronizing cursor never advances the watermark and schedules a snapshot, a cursor that fails closed throws, and backwards or undelivered-claiming acks are rejected.
- **Authority adapters (#30).** `DeviceGrantAuthority` is the only source of scope; unknown, revoked, and expired devices collapse onto `notObservable` because the session layer already holds the device's liveness, while every other authority failure propagates so an unavailable authority denies disclosure with no fallback (invariant 15). `CodexBridgeAssembly` supplies the unfiltered snapshot, and that is the only unfiltered read on the path — its result never leaves the broker unfiltered.
- **Read-cursor storage model (#31).** `DeviceReadCursorStore` is the storage half of the Step 2.9 `markThreadRead` command; **no network mutation and no Codex call exists on this path**. Three rules are enforced in the store rather than by a caller: device-own (every operation takes the authenticated device ID and touches only that device's entries), scoped (a position is disclosed or advanced only for a thread inside the device's current Mac-stored project scope, re-read every call; a position for a thread that has left scope is retained but never disclosed, so restoring scope does not lose the mark while reducing it does not leak that the thread exists), and monotonic (a regressing *or repeated* value is rejected rather than silently ignored, so a replayed command cannot quietly unread a thread). Writes persist before they become visible through a versioned canonical blob with the same contract as the grant authority's; `FileBackedReadCursorStorage` stores it under Application Support with a `0700` directory, `0600` file, atomic replacement, and backup exclusion. Absence is a valid fresh state here, unlike the grant authority — losing read cursors loses UI state, not a security property, and that difference is documented at the seam.
- **Linearizable authorization changes (#32).** `AuthorizationChangeCoordinator` performs every change in the one order that makes it linearizable: **commit** in the grant authority, **purge** the device's queued and retained observation data and its now-unauthorized read positions, then **report** what the transport must do. The order is structural rather than a convention — the actor is the only place the three steps appear together and each step's result feeds the next. Because the commit happens first and every reader re-reads the authority rather than caching it, an operation either completed under the prior authorization before the commit or is denied after it. Terminal changes report `close(.deviceRevoked)`/`close(.grantExpired)`; retaining changes report `reauthenticate`, except that losing `.view` ends observation and closes. `expire` is the API the active-session expiry scheduler must call, persisting the tombstone before the affected sessions close. The coordinator deliberately does **not** close connections: the transport owns those, so the caller that owns both applies the returned action.
- **Sealed post-authentication carriage (`MacBridgeServer`, #33).** `ListenerApplicationKind` is the post-authentication allowlist and is as narrow as the handshake one — exactly two device-originated kinds out of four, with no command, approval, or attachment kind represented at all, so the Step 2.9 gateway cannot be reached through this surface. `ListenerObservationHandler` sits tail-most: behind the gate, so no unauthenticated byte reaches a frame codec, and behind every ceiling, so sealed traffic is metered exactly like handshake traffic. It opens every inbound frame and seals every outbound delivery with the session's directional keys and adds no tolerance on top of the Step 2.3 codec's exactly-next rules. Frame-codec **ownership transfers exactly once** — the counters must never be advanced from two places — so `CoordinatorListenerHandshakeHandler` stores them and the connection claims them. Post-authentication reasons are specific, unlike the collapsed pre-authentication reason, because the peer has proven it is the device the reason is about. A build with no wired frame provider keeps the Step 2.7 behaviour (admitted, counted, kept alive, nothing delivered) but is inert rather than permissive: it closes on the first application message it cannot open.

Nothing in Step 2.8 owns both the broker and the listener, so no component wakes a connection when `recordThreadChange` reports new authorized data; the broker returns the woken device IDs and the transport exposes the delivery path, but wiring the push — like the Step 2.5/2.6 sweep timers — is Step 2.13 assembly work. Until then a device learns of changes only when it next speaks.
- **Nothing calls `recordThreadChange` yet.** `CodexBridgeAssembly` conforms to the snapshot seam, but its event pump does not yet notify the broker; the assembly that connects them is Step 2.13.
- **No command path.** `markThreadRead` and `interruptTurn` remain Step 2.9: this step delivers the read-cursor **storage model** only, and the post-authentication allowlist deliberately has no command kind at all. `sendPrompt`/`steerTurn` are P1 and approvals stay rejected.
- **The authorization-change coordinator does not close connections.** It returns the action; the component that owns both the coordinator and the listener applies it. That component does not exist yet, so "closes every affected connection" is proven as far as *deciding and purging*, not as far as a socket closing.
- **Ordering is proven on one side.** The hard guarantee — every operation started after the commit is denied — is asserted directly. An operation that began before the commit may still complete under the prior authorization; that is what the invariant permits, and the tests do not (and cannot, without instrumentation) pin the interleaving of an in-flight read.
- **Approval content is excluded by decision, not deferred by accident.** Devices observe threads and turn status only. Binding an approval surface belongs with the Phase 4 device-bound user-presence assertion.
- **The read-cursor file store's production path is exercised**, unlike the Keychain paths, because it needs no entitlement — but it is exercised in a temporary directory, not in a real user Application Support container under sandboxing.
- Source-only and loopback evidence: no simulator, generic-device, or physical-device claim is made by Step 2.8. The generic iOS device build of `CompanionProtocol`/`CompanionCrypto` was not run in this environment and remains required merge evidence.

## Step 2.9 outcome — central command gateway, interrupt, and read cursor

### Implemented scope

Merged as three stacked PRs (#35–#37); no new dependency, target, package, Bonjour advertisement, or user-facing control was added, and `MacBridgeServer` still does not import `MacBridgeCore`.

- **Semantic command digest (#35).** `SemanticCommandDigest` covers exactly the command type, normalized target, payload, and effective policy, and deliberately excludes the command ID (that is the ledger *key*), `issuedAt`, session IDs, and frame counters (plan §2 invariant 12). It is **not** `CommandFingerprint`: that Phase 1 digest hashes the whole `ClientCommand` including `issuedAt`, so an honest retry after a reconnect would look like a digest collision and fail closed on a device that did nothing wrong. Encoding is canonical — version byte, domain separator, length-prefixed fields in fixed order — so no two distinct commands collide by concatenation. Both ledgers gained a `claim` method taking the digest explicitly.
- **The gateway and its fixed order (#35).** `NetworkCommandGateway` is the sole network→core semantic mutation path. Every command runs: current session → current Mac authority → closed surfaces → semantic digest → **known-result read** → freshness, project scope, capability, mobile profile, runtime readiness → **ledger claim** → single external call. Approvals are rejected for all of Phase 2 (invariant 17), nonempty attachments are rejected (invariant 16), and every kind outside `{interruptTurn, markThreadRead}` is rejected (plan §9) — all three before any ledger record exists, so a rejected command leaves no trace.
- **Two orderings are load-bearing, and one was found while building it.** Disclosure authorization runs *before* a known result is returned, so a device whose scope was reduced after issuing a command cannot read the answer back. And the known-result **read** precedes the **claim**: the first draft claimed before the policy checks, which meant a command denied for `runtimeUnavailable` left a claimed-but-unfinished record — so an honest retry after the runtime recovered would have found its own denied claim and resolved to `outcomeUnknown` instead of executing. The plan orders the claim last ("…runtime readiness, and ledger claim before one external call"); that is now what the code does and what a test pins.
- **`interruptTurn` (#36).** Requires the explicit `.interrupt` capability and makes **at most one** `turn/interrupt` call across duplicates, retries, and reconnects. The single call is guaranteed by the claim rather than by the executor: a duplicate never reaches execution because the known-result read returned first. The ledger moves to `submitted` *before* the call, so a crash on either side of it is ambiguous in the safe direction, and a transport failure is terminal — automatic retries are disabled (invariant 13).
- **`markThreadRead` (#36).** **Never calls Codex.** The position is device-local UI state, so a degraded runtime does not deny it and no external call is made under any outcome. The Step 2.8 read-cursor store enforces device-own, scoped, and monotonic itself, so a regressing or repeated position is a *denial* rather than a silent no-op — a replayed command cannot quietly unread a thread.
- **Sealed command carriage (#37).** Two new application kinds, `commandRequest` and `commandResult`, bring the post-authentication inbound allowlist to exactly three. The transport carries **one opaque command envelope, not per-command wire kinds**: it decodes strictly, hands the command to `ListenerCommandHandling` with the identity that came from authentication, and seals the closed result back. Which commands are permitted is decided entirely behind the seam, so no future wire change there can enable a command the gateway has not accepted, and the transport can name no executor, runtime, or ledger. The seam returns a small closed outcome rather than `SecureCommandResult`, so the fallible wire value is constructed in exactly one place where a failure closes the connection instead of being swallowed.

### Step 2.9 verification

**53 new deterministic tests** (root suite total **806**, up from 753): 42 in `MacBridgeCoreTests` (279 → 321) and 11 in `MacBridgeServerTests` (228 → 239).

- **At most one external call** is the central claim and is asserted by *counting calls* against a fake responder in every duplicate, reconnect, failure, denial, and crash-boundary test — never inferred from ledger state. Five duplicate submissions produce one call; a reconnect with a new session and fresh `issuedAt` produces one call and replays the recorded result; a failed call becomes `outcomeUnknown` and is never resent even after the responder recovers; missing capability, out-of-scope project, and a degraded runtime each produce **zero** calls.

- **Known-result replay:** a terminal result replays without executing, replays past an elapsed freshness window and a degraded runtime, and is **withheld** with `projectNotAllowed` when scope was reduced after the fact.
- **Device-bound identity:** command-ID reuse with a different payload and cross-device reuse are both `duplicateMismatch`; an honest retry with a fresh `issuedAt` is **not** a collision.
- **Denial coverage:** stale session, another device's session identifier, a revoked device, an unavailable authority, a failing ledger, out-of-scope project, unattributed thread, missing capability, stale command, and a degraded runtime — each a closed reason, each leaving no queued work. ### Honest limitations

- **The gateway is not wired into a running bridge.** Its session verifier, runtime readiness, responder, and read-cursor store are seams; the component that adapts `SessionCoordinator` to `NetworkSessionVerifying` and hands the gateway to the listener is Step 2.13 assembly work, exactly like the Step 2.8 observation wiring. A live probe requires explicit confirmation and has not been run.
- **The interrupt has no completion reconciliation.** Unlike the Phase 1 approval executor, which waits for `serverRequest/resolved`, the gateway treats a returned `turn/interrupt` call as success. If Codex accepts the call but does not act, the ledger records `succeeded`. Binding an interrupt to an observed turn-state change is not Phase 2 scope.

In practice the session layer closes such a connection first; the gateway's denial is a backstop, and its reason is deliberately conservative rather than precise.

## Step 2.10 outcome — `sendPrompt` through the same gateway

### Implemented scope

- **`PhoneTurnPolicy`.** The bridge-resolved settings for one phone-originated turn: sandbox, bridge-capped writable roots, network access, and approval policy. **Every field is decided on the Mac.** That is not a convention — `ClientCommandBody.sendPrompt` carries exactly a thread ID, a prompt, and attachments, so there is no phone-supplied path, sandbox, approval, or network value to ignore; a test asserts the encoded command body's field set is exactly `{type, threadID, prompt, attachmentIDs}`.

- **`CodexTurnStarting`.** A separate seam from `CodexApprovalResponding` because it is a different authority: responding to an approval answers a request Codex already raised, while starting a turn originates work. `CodexRuntimeSession` gained `startTurn`, `LiveCodexRuntimeSession` builds the `turn/start` parameters from the policy, and `CodexRuntimeSupervisor` forwards through `readySession()`, which refuses unless the runtime is `ready`.

The existing `CapabilityPolicy` then applies the profile ceiling: `observe` and `respond` are denied `actionProfileTooRestrictive`, and the host profile caps the device profile.
- **Ledger ordering.** Unlike the interrupt, the turn identifier is unknown until the call returns, so the ledger is marked `submitted` **after** it, with the turn ID. Both orders leave a non-terminal record if the bridge stops mid-flight, and a non-terminal record is crash-ambiguous either way — it resolves to `outcomeUnknown` and is never resent.

### A test-harness defect found and fixed during Step 2.10

Step 2.8's embedded-channel test helper called `EmbeddedEventLoop.run()` after an `await`, so it ran from whichever thread resumed the task. `EmbeddedEventLoop` is single-threaded by contract, and NIO reported the misuse **19,081 times per suite run** while warning it will become a forced crash.

It was not only noise. Once Step 2.10's new tests shifted the interleaving, the race became a real deadlock: `swift test` hung inside `ListenerAssembledPipelineTests` and never completed. The three affected suites now use `NIOAsyncTestingChannel`, the async-safe equivalent (#39, #40), and the misuse count is 3 for a whole run.

Two things are worth recording honestly. First, the first fix (#39) converted two suites, cut the count to 17, and I described the residue as benign — **that was wrong**, and the very next branch deadlocked in the same place; #40 corrected it. Second, the underlying cause is a production pattern, not only a test one: `ListenerObservationHandler` hands work to a `Task` and hops back through `eventLoop.execute`, which is correct for a real event loop but is a cross-thread call into an embedded one. Any future suite that drives an `EmbeddedChannel` through a pipeline containing that handler will hit the same race, so new listener tests must use `NIOAsyncTestingChannel`.

## Step 2.11 outcome — `steerTurn` proven against the turn's own policy

### Implemented scope

- **`TurnPolicyRegistry` and `RecordedTurnPolicy`.** Every turn the gateway starts is recorded with the effective profile it actually runs under and the exact `PhoneTurnPolicy` it was started with. This is the authoritative state steering is proven against — the plan's requirement is to deny steering when the turn's policy "cannot be proven from authoritative state", and an unrecorded turn is precisely that.
- **Two refusals, in order.** `policyUnknown` for a turn the bridge cannot prove anything about, and `policyBroaderThanDevice` for a turn running under a profile broader than the device's *current* effective profile. Equal or narrower policies are steerable. Both refusals reach the wire as `actionProfileTooRestrictive` and both make **zero** external calls.
- **Memory-only by design.** A bridge restart loses every record, so every pre-restart turn becomes unsteerable. Persisting would be worse, not better: a stale record would outlive the runtime that could honour it, and the phone would be told a turn is steerable under a policy no live turn is running.
- **Bounded.** The registry keeps the most recent 512 turns and evicts the oldest. An evicted turn becomes unprovable and is refused — never assumed.
- **`CodexTurnSteering`.** A seam separate from `CodexTurnStarting`, because the authority differs: starting a turn *chooses* a policy while steering may only continue one that already exists. The seam carries a target and text and **no policy field at all**, so steering cannot widen a turn even if a caller wanted it to. `ClientCommandBody.steerTurn` likewise carries exactly `{threadID, turnID, prompt}`.
- **The `runAgent` gate still applies first.** Steering requires the same explicit capability `sendPrompt` does, and the same profile ceiling, so a device without `runAgent` is denied `capabilityMissing` before the turn's policy is ever consulted. `steerTurn` is therefore gated three times: allowlist, grant, and the turn's own recorded policy.

### Step 2.11 verification

**19 new deterministic tests**, every one counting steer calls against a fake steerer.

- **The decision rule directly:** an unrecorded turn is `policyUnknown`; a `runWorkspace` turn is refused for a `runReadOnly` device and allowed for a `runWorkspace` one; a `runReadOnly` turn is steerable by a `runWorkspace` device; the registry is bounded and evicted turns become unprovable; re-recording the same turn replaces rather than grows.
- **Through the gateway:** a turn this bridge started is steerable; a turn it never started is refused with zero calls; **a turn broader than the device's current profile is refused even though the same device started it** — the grant was narrowed to `runReadOnly` after a `runWorkspace` turn began; and another device with an equal profile may steer.
- **The earlier gates still bite:** no `runAgent` → `capabilityMissing`; an `observe` profile → the ceiling; a revoked device; a degraded runtime. All with zero calls.
- **Idempotency and crash boundaries:** five duplicates → one call; a reconnect → one call plus a replay; a failed steer is `outcomeUnknown` and never resent even after the steerer recovers; a crash between claim and steer resolves to `outcomeUnknown` with zero calls; a different steer prompt under the same command ID is `duplicateMismatch`.
- **The seam cannot widen a turn:** what it received is exactly a target and text, and the recorded policy is unchanged afterwards; the command schema's body field set is exactly `{type, threadID, turnID, prompt}`.

### Honest limitations

- **The `turn/steer` method name is unverified and weaker evidence than `sendPrompt`'s.** `turn/start` and `turn/interrupt` both appear in the merged Phase 0 spike against a real app-server; **`turn/steer` appears nowhere in this repository's proven surface.** It is written to the documented architecture and has never been sent to a running Codex. A wrong method name surfaces as a failed call — which is `outcomeUnknown`, not a silent success — but it means the steering path may not work at all until a live probe confirms the method. That probe must happen before Step 2.13.
- **No live steer was executed.** Every result is against an injected `CodexTurnSteering` double.
- **A turn started in the IDE is never steerable from a phone** in Phase 2, because the bridge has no record of its policy. That is the plan's own rule rather than an oversight, but it is a real product consequence: the phone can steer only turns it started.
- **A bridge restart makes every existing turn unsteerable.** Also the safe direction, also a real functional gap.
- **The registry does not track turn completion.** A finished turn keeps its record until evicted, so steering a completed turn is attempted and fails at Codex, arriving as `outcomeUnknown` rather than a clean denial. Binding the registry to observed `turn/completed` events would fix it and is not Phase 2 scope.
- **Workspace-write steering is untested in practice** for the same reason it is unreachable in Step 2.10: with no production writable-root resolver, every bridge-started turn is read-only, so the broader-policy refusal is proven against injected profiles rather than real workspace turns.

## Step 2.12 outcome — `startThread` deferred by recorded decision

### The decision

**Codex Micro v1 is read/respond/control-only: the phone may not create threads.** `startThread` is deferred to a later phase rather than implemented behind a Mac feature toggle.

Recorded 2026-08-03 by the project owner at the plan's own checkpoint. The plan (§4, Step 2.12) makes this a product decision and states that, once deferred, "this step is not required for P0 completion, provisional completion, or Phase 2 acceptance." Plan §8 already lists `startThread` under explicitly deferred scope on exactly this condition.

### Why this was the recommendation

- **It is the highest-surface P1 command.** `sendPrompt` and `steerTurn` continue work in a thread the Mac already knows about; `startThread` originates new agent work in a project, which is a strictly larger authority.
- **It could not function today anyway.** It needs an opaque project ID resolved to a real workspace, and no production resolver exists — the same gap that keeps thread attribution fail-closed and makes workspace-write unreachable. A shipped `startThread` would be denied `projectNotAllowed` in practice, so implementing it would add reviewable surface and no capability.
- **Deferring costs nothing downstream.** Milestone 2 (Mac/protocol complete) explicitly admits a deferred 2.12, and the acceptance gate does not test it.

The decision is enforced by a test, not only recorded in prose. `startThread` stays off `NetworkCommandGateway.allowedCommandKinds`, and `testStartThreadStaysClosedByTheRecordedStepTwelveDeferral` asserts that a grant holding **every** capability — including `.startThread` — is denied `unsupportedCommand` under **every** mobile action profile, and leaves no ledger record. Re-enabling the command therefore requires deliberately deleting that test, which is the moment the decision should be revisited.

### Honest limitations

- **This is a decision, not an implementation.** No `startThread` contract, project-resolution path, or forced-profile logic exists to review; a later phase that revives it starts from the plan's Step 2.12 requirements, not from partial code.
- **The deferral is scoped to v1.** Nothing here argues `startThread` is unsafe in principle — only that it is out of scope now and would be inert if built now.

## Step 2.13 outcome — Mac controls, Bonjour, and the running assembly

Step 2.13 is the step that turns a proven set of contracts into a bridge that can actually run. It landed as eight PRs (#46–#53).

### What landed

- **The unauthenticated-availability defect is closed (#46).** The ADR fixes 4 concurrent unauthenticated connections globally and says nothing about their distribution, so those 4 were also the total number of simultaneous peers that could be mid-handshake. Two tightenings close it, both leaving the ADR's numbers untouched: a per-source share of the global ceiling, and a silence budget bounding a connection that holds a slot without sending anything. Every accepted message rearms the budget, so a peer making progress is never cut off early. Three existing tests asserted that a silent connection survives to the 20-second deadline — that *was* the defect, so they now assert the new contract.
- **Production Bonjour and its lifecycle (#47).** The record is entirely compile-time constants and carries no host, device, user, identifier, fingerprint, interface, project, or secret value — **not filtered out, but absent**, because the type exposes no way to supply one. Two Macs publish byte-identical records. The coordinator owns three orderings: advertise after readiness, roll the listener back when publication fails, remove the record before closing.
- **The adapters joining the three targets (#48).** Phase 2 kept `MacBridgeServer`, `MacBridgeCore`, and `CompanionCrypto` apart with a protocol at every seam; one file now satisfies them, so the executor-bypass rule holds by construction — there is exactly one file to review for it. A revoked or expired device keeps its distinct closed liveness rather than collapsing onto "unknown device", and remaining grant lifetime crosses as a duration so no wall-clock movement can extend a live session.
- **The LAN enable control and authorization enforcer (#49).** The controller owns the order of operations: bind then advertise, roll back on advertisement failure, remove then close on disable, never report `enabled` after a failed enable, and build a fresh listener on re-enable because the hardened lifecycle is one-shot. The enforcer is what was missing between the authorization-change coordinator and real connections.
- **Project resolution (#51)** — the largest functional gap in Phase 2, now closed. Thread attribution and writable roots were both fail-closed defaults, so a running bridge would have disclosed nothing and denied every thread-targeted command. `thread["cwd"]` is proven against a live app-server by the merged Phase 0 spike, so a thread resolves to a project the Mac user allowlisted. The device sees a digest of the canonical root path, never the path. Containment is checked on **path boundaries**, because a bare prefix test would silently widen a project's scope to a sibling directory, and the most specific project wins so a nested project attributes its own threads. A project's only writable root is its own directory.
- **The journal-driven pump and sweep scheduler (#52).** The pump replays from its own cursor to recover thread identifiers and **resolves attribution before telling the broker** — the broker reads attribution synchronously and an unattributed thread is invisible, so resolving second would silently drop the first change to every new thread. A retention gap is counted and the cursor jumps rather than inventing deliveries. The scheduler runs the sweeps Steps 2.5 and 2.6 documented as opportunistic and left for the assembly.
- **The user-facing surface (#53).** The SAS word list, deferred twice since Step 2.3, is **generated rather than transcribed**: a hand-copied natural-language list is 2,048 chances for a silent typo and one wrong entry makes two honest devices disagree about an identical phrase. Redacted metrics carry counts and closed states only. The LAN menu control is the only way to turn the listener on and is off on every launch — enablement is never persisted, because a bridge that silently re-enables itself is a listener the user did not ask for this time. `Info.plist` carries the ADR §12 local-network keys.

## Step 2.14 progress — the composition root

Step 2.13 recorded that "no component news up the whole graph" and that closing it would be "small and mechanical". It was neither, and the reason is the finding below.

### The wiring defect

`HardenedWSSListener.makeBootstrap` called `configureChild` **without** an `observation`, `commands`, or `frameProvider` argument. All three parameters had denying defaults, so every listener the actor built served the denying handlers — no observation, no commands, and no way for an authenticated connection to open a single application message. `ListenerConfiguration` had no field to inject them either, so there was no way to fix it at a call site.

Steps 2.8 and 2.9 were tested against `ListenerPipeline.configure`, which does take all three. Only tests called it. Everything the phase built on top of those seams was correct and unreachable.

This is the specific thing a composition root is for. No unit test could have caught it, because the defect was an *absent* argument at the one call site no test exercised, and the assertion that would have failed could not be written — there was no field to assert on.

### What landed

- **The three seams are now on `ListenerConfiguration`** and threaded through `makeBootstrap` to `configureChild`. All three keep denying defaults, so a configuration built without them still serves nothing rather than serving unfiltered state.
- **A production enabled-configuration factory.** Before this, `testOnlyEnabled` was the only factory producing `isEnabled: true` — the shipping app had no route to a bound socket. `ListenerConfiguration.enabled(by:…)` requires a `ListenerEnablement`, which has no public initializer, no `Codable` conformance, and no stored state, so it cannot be decoded from a preference or restored from disk. The only way to obtain one is `ListenerEnablement.userRequested()`, which makes every production enable greppable. Production also defaults to the strict interface policy, so loopback is refused.
- **`BridgeNetworkAssembly`** — the composition root. It builds the TLS provider over the Secure Enclave `.tls` identity, the three startup probes, the handshake handler, and the listener, and vends the `BridgeLANController` the menu drives. It owns no policy; every decision it makes is a wiring decision.
- **A fresh frame registry per listener**, and the *same* registry on both sides of the handshake. Codecs carry counters, so a registry resumed across LAN sessions is a replay window; two different registries would authenticate a device that could then open nothing.
- **The startup probes are real.** The grant probe fails LAN closed when the authority is latched. The Codex probe refuses an unsupported version, a schema mismatch, and a probe that could not run. The policy probe checks the one property `authorize` leans on — that combining every action profile lands on `observe` — and deliberately does not reach for the fileprivate `permitsAgentWork` to do it.

### Step 2.14 verification so far

**13 new deterministic tests** (root suite total **964**, up from 951). Three of them assert the wiring directly: real handlers rather than denying ones, one shared frame registry, and a distinct registry per listener.

### What Step 2.14 still owes

- The iOS acceptance host does not exist. There is no `.xcodeproj` and no app target, so nothing has been signed or installed.
- Nothing has bound a socket or published a Bonjour record. The assembly is proven by construction, not by a bind.
- The Secure Enclave identity is still not bound to the pairing and session signer seams, and `DeviceGrantAuthority.addGrant` is still not called with a pairing proposal.
- The pairing QR and phrase confirmation still have no UI.
- `turn/steer` and the `workspaceWrite` form of `turn/start` remain unverified against a live Codex.
